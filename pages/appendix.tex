\section{The smoothing filter and its removal}\label{sec:alpha-ablation}

An earlier version of the oracle did not compare its thresholds against
the live dirty fraction $d_k$ but against an exponentially weighted moving
average $\hat{d}_k = \alpha d_k + (1-\alpha)\hat{d}_{k-1}$, with the
smoothing factor $\alpha \in [0,1]$ as a third parameter and a default of
$0.2$. These are the measurements behind the removal that
Section~\ref{sec:approach-nofilter} states as a design decision. They sit
here rather than in Chapter~\ref{chapter:experiments} because the
parameter no longer exists in the simulator, so no result there can be
reproduced with it; the runs are archived under
\texttt{experiments-old-ewma-0.2/}. The sweep covers
$\alpha \in \{0.0, 0.1, \dots, 1.0\}$ at $R \in \{1, 5, 10, 15\}$ across
three seeds, 132 runs, at $c = 10\%$ and $r = 5\%$.

Table~\ref{tab:alpha-grid} puts the earlier default $\alpha = 0.2$ next to
the filter disabled at $\alpha = 1.0$, which is the same code path reading
the live count. Its $p_{95}$ columns are the tail of the dirty fraction
against the configured $c$, and $\tilde{d}_{\mathrm{exit}}$ is the median live
dirty fraction at the return to BASE, against the configured $r$. The
degenerate $\alpha = 0$ row is left out. It freezes the estimate at an
empty dirty set, so the run never leaves BASE and reproduces the X1 BASE
throughput to five significant figures, which confirms that $\alpha$
reached the oracle.

\paragraph{The filter missed only the exit edge.} Across the engaged range
of $\alpha$ the measured $p_{95}$ stays between 9.34\% and 11.11\% against
the configured $c$. Entry is decided in BASE, where admissions are
frequent and the dirty fraction rises slowly, so even a heavily lagged
estimate crosses $c$ close to the right moment. Exit is the opposite case.
In ACID admissions are throttled and the dirty set drains fast, so the
smoothed estimate stays above $r$ long after the true fraction has fallen
below it, and the oracle leaves ACID at 1.95\% at $R = 1$ and 0.00\% at
$R = 15$ against a configured 5\%. Each ACID visit runs 36 to 95\% longer
as a result. Disabling the filter moves those exits to 3.62\% and 1.52\%,
closer to $r$ at every $R$, leaving the sampling interval of
Section~\ref{sec:effective-recovery} as the only remaining cause of the
gap.

\paragraph{It did not calm the system either.} The filter was introduced
as an anti-flap measure. It lowers the raw switch count by 9 to 24\%, but
the mean time spent in BASE per entry moves by between $-7$\% and $+7$\%
at $R = 5$, 10 and 15, that is, not at all to within the seed dispersion.
Fewer switches in a fixed run length is a consequence of longer ACID
visits rather than calmer ones, and the hysteresis band is what does the
anti-flap work.

\paragraph{Scope of the comparison.} The sweep varies $\alpha$ alone
inside one build, so it establishes the effect of the filter.
Chapter~\ref{chapter:experiments} was then re-run on five seeds against
the filter-free implementation and the entry edge agrees, the $p_{95}$ at
$c = 0.10$ moving by at most 0.26 percentage points. Nothing else should
be read across the two builds, which differ in more than $\alpha$.
