\chapter{\abstractname}

The abstract will be written last, in about 200 words, and will follow the
structure below.

Multi-model engineering systems keep several representations of the same
product in agreement through automated consistency-preserving updates.
Scheduling that work inside the transaction (ACID) keeps the models
consistent at a high throughput cost, while deferring it to background
reconcilers (BASE) is fast but leaves the models inconsistent by an amount
the operator cannot choose. This thesis adds a hybrid execution mode that
decides between the two per transaction, at admission time, and an
adaptive oracle that makes that decision from the measured dirty fraction.
The abstract will state the correctness problem the mixture creates and
the arbiter that resolves it, then give the two headline measurements: the
configured threshold acts as a bound on the inconsistency tail to within
1.3 percentage points across a tenfold range of the threshold and a
fifteenfold range of reconciler provisioning, and this bound comes from
the feedback loop rather than from mixing the two modes, since a fixed
random split needs recalibration for every deployment. It will close on
the honest summary: the adaptive oracle does not make the ACID/BASE
trade-off cheaper, it makes it addressable.
